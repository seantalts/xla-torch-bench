# XLA:CPU vs PyTorch Inductor — perf-win opportunities (consolidated)

Source: 98-row benchmark sweep on 2026-06-04, Apple M3 Ultra, 8 threads (XLA properly capped via `PJRT_NPROC`/`NPROC` for the first time — earlier runs had XLA at 32 cores and PyTorch at 8, biasing in XLA's favor). Supersedes [`perf-wins-2026-06-02.md`](perf-wins-2026-06-02.md) and [`perf-wins-ynn-followup-2026-06-02.md`](perf-wins-ynn-followup-2026-06-02.md) — those remain useful for the YNN-REDUCE specific findings but their absolute speedup numbers are now stale.

## Summary

Three aggregate findings after broadening from 14 → 38 distinct benchmarks across 9 categories:

1. **Sort family is the worst-performing category found anywhere.** `argsort_axis` 0.03x, `sort_full` 0.05x, `bucketize` 0.15x f32, and a bf16 `topk_logits` that's 0.01x at *constant ~5.7ms regardless of `k`* — strongly suggests an algorithmic fallback (likely full sort or scalar) for bf16 topk on CPU. **None of this was on the radar in the prior perf-wins doc**.
2. **f32 GEMM is broader and worse than measured before.** With proper threading, `dot_medium f32` is 0.16x (was 0.43x at uncapped XLA) and the gap cascades through every transformer benchmark — `gpt2_block 0.31–0.46x`, `mha_block 0.32–0.39x`, `gqa_block 0.39x`. The original "GEMM is the dominant cost" diagnosis from `perf-wins-2026-06-02.md` is correct and its priority just moved up.
3. **Indexing/gather and MoE primitives are XLA strengths, not weaknesses.** The prior assumption that XLA gather is slow turns out to be wrong on this machine — `embedding_lookup` 3.7x bf16, `dynamic_slice_loop` 3.0x f32, `segment_sum` 5.4x f32, `top2_router` 3.2x. Worth confirming on Linux x86 before generalizing, but the suspected gather/scatter pain points are not active gaps here.

## Ranked opportunities (post-threading-fix)

Ranking by `(payoff × confidence) / effort`, with payoff weighted by how broadly the fix would propagate.

### 1. f32 GEMM — the dominant compounding loss (Apple-specific cause confirmed)
**Gap.** `dot_medium (1024³) f32` = **0.16x** (XLA 2.66ms vs torch 0.42ms — ~6.3× slower). `dot_transpose 0.27x`, `dot_bias_gelu 0.47x`, `dot_small 0.32x`. Every transformer benchmark inherits this: `mha_block 0.32–0.39x`, `gqa_block 0.39x`, `gpt2_block 0.31–0.46x`, `mlp_mixer 0.58x`.

**Root cause on Apple Silicon (verified, see [arm-dispatch investigation](#arm-dispatch-finding) below):** On macOS aarch64, `XLA_ONEDNN` is undefined and the oneDNN dispatch path is dead code. f32 dots route to **YNNPACK** (`__ynn_fusion` custom kernel → XNNPACK NEON GEMM). YNN ≈ Eigen ≈ ~800 GFLOPS on M3 Ultra (verified by cross-bench). PyTorch hits ~5 TFLOPS via `Accelerate.framework`/BNNS → Apple AMX coprocessor. **The gap is "no AMX backend in XLA," not a dispatch or codegen quality issue.** No NEON-only library can close this gap.

**What this means for the codegen story:** The dot_medium ratio cannot be improved by tuning the YNN/Eigen NEON kernels — they're already at NEON-FMA roofline. Closing the Apple gap is a new-backend integration project (link `Accelerate.framework`, add a `use_accelerate` flag analogous to `use_acl`, route `DotImplementationStrategy::kEigen` → Accelerate when targeting Apple Silicon). Substantial, Apple-only, out of perf-wins scope.

**The x86 GEMM story is separately interesting and unverified.** On Linux x86_64, `XLA_ONEDNN` is defined and the dispatch path is completely different (real oneDNN). The 0.16x ratio is an Apple-only number; x86 dot_medium vs torch could be anywhere from in-line to materially better/worse. **A Linux x86 sweep is the critical next data point** before any x86-targeted GEMM work.

**Effort: L** (new backend) for Apple. **Unknown** for x86 until measured.
**Payoff:** transformer-wide on Apple; unknown size on x86.
**Confidence: very high** that this is the cause on Apple. **N/A** on x86.

### 2. Sort / topk / argsort — single biggest category-level gap
**Gap.** `argsort_axis (1024,512) f32` = **0.03x** (30× slower). `sort_full (1024,4096) f32` = **0.05x**. `bucketize 0.15x` f32. bf16 topk constant ~5.7ms regardless of `k`.

**Why it's slow.** Two distinct issues:
- **bf16 topk is constant-time at 5.7ms across `k` ∈ {10, 100, 1000}**. This is the single most diagnostic data point in the whole sweep — it's an algorithmic fallback, not a constant-factor codegen issue. XLA likely converts bf16 → f32, runs a full sort, then partial-selects. Or there's no bf16 topk path at all and it falls to a scalar reference impl.
- **Sort/argsort/bucketize are generic comparator loops.** XLA's HLO `sort` lowers to a generic emitter with no SIMD. PyTorch/Inductor has vectorized small-array sort kernels.

**What to try.**
- **bf16 topk dispatch fix (~1 day).** Pre-convert bf16→f32 in the topk lowering when bf16 specialization is missing. Even with the convert overhead, beats 5.7ms massively.
- **Vectorized sort emitter (M–L).** A bitonic small-row sort for the inner axis with a thread-parallel outer loop would close most of the gap. SIMD merge-sort would too. Both are well-trod literature.
- **Branchless bucketize (S).** Vectorized binary-search on a small (256-element) sorted boundary array fits in L1 easily; the gap is pure emitter quality.

**Effort: S (bf16 topk), S (bucketize), M–L (full sort emitter).**
**Payoff: very high on the bf16 topk** (80× immediate). **Medium-high on sort/argsort/bucketize** (depends on workload; matters for LLM sampling, retrieval, masking).
**Confidence: high** on diagnosis (constant-time bf16 topk is unambiguous).

### 3. YNN REDUCE wraps reductions before instruction fusion — blocks downstream loop fusion
**Gap.** `layer_norm (1,4096,768) f32` = **0.27x** (XLA 1.52ms vs torch 0.42ms). `sum_axis`/`mean_axis` 0.74–0.81x f32. Disabling YNN REDUCE gives 3× on layer_norm and 20% on sum/mean_axis (see [YNN toggle data](perf-wins-ynn-followup-2026-06-02.md)).

**Root cause (verified via source review, see [YNN-reduce investigation](#ynn-reduce-finding) below):** `LibraryRewriter` runs at `xla/service/cpu/cpu_compiler.cc:1049-1054`, **before** `CpuInstructionFusion` at line 1059. When YNN REDUCE wraps a reduce as a `__ynn_fusion` custom fusion, `CpuInstructionFusion::ComputeInstructionsToSkip` (`xla/service/cpu/cpu_instruction_fusion.cc:294`) adds every `IsCustomFusion()` to the skip set. The downstream regular instruction-fusion pass therefore can no longer fuse the reduce with its elementwise consumers — the divide-by-N in `mean_axis`, the broadcast-then-subtract chain in `layer_norm`, etc. The existing exclusion in `IsReduceLikeOpOffloadedToYnn` (`xla/backends/cpu/ynn_support.cc:475-501`) only checks the reduce's **input** opcode (kMultiply, kBroadcast, kSlice, kConcatenate), not the consumer. Most reductions in transformer-shape workloads feed elementwise chains, so they get wrapped and isolated.

**Direct evidence of team awareness:** `xla/backends/cpu/transforms/ynn_matcher.h:78` — `TODO(b/441837668): Need to get the reduction performance right before enabling fusions.` And `ynn_support.cc:484-491` — `// For now they are excluded because the codegen for the fusion with reduce can be faster.`

**Resolves the rms_norm "wins" puzzle**: rms_norm's reduce has `kMultiply` as its input (`mean(x*x)` lowers to `reduce(multiply(x,x), 0, add)`), so the existing input-opcode filter rejects it — YNN REDUCE never fires on rms_norm. The 17-33% delta in the toggle data is secondary ELTWISE-fusion noise, not a YNN-REDUCE contribution. So the toggle-on-for-rms_norm-small case isn't actually a constraint on the fix.

**What to try (in order of effort/safety):**
- **Conservative heuristic in `IsReduceLikeOpOffloadedToYnn`**: additionally reject when `hlo->user_count() == 1 && hlo->users().front()->IsElementwise() && !hlo->users().front()->IsConstant()`. Lands the layer_norm 3× and most of the sum/mean_axis 20% on this machine. Needs **measurement on x86** before commit because YNN's reduce kernel quality on AVX-512 may differ from M3 NEON. The rms_norm-small bf16-softmax "wins" from the toggle data aren't a constraint here (toggle effect is structural-fusion-noise, not YNN-REDUCE-doing-the-work).
- **Pass reordering** (move `CpuInstructionFusion` before `LibraryRewriter`): bigger change; requires YNN matcher to recognize and unwrap pre-existing fusions. Higher reward, much higher risk.
- **File an upstream issue** with the dsharlet@/ashaposhnikov@/penporn@ team citing the YNN-followup data and source trace. They already TODO'd this — the right people may already be working on it.

**Effort: S** (heuristic) — **M+** (pass reorder).
**Payoff: high.** Layer_norm 3× directly. Cascades through gpt2_block / mlp_mixer_block which both contain 2× LN per block. ~20% on standalone sum/mean_axis.
**Confidence: very high** (root cause is source-traced, perf delta is toggle-confirmed).

### 4. bf16 reduction emitter — vectorized accumulator on widen-on-load
**Gap.** `sum_axis bf16 0.33x`, `mean_axis bf16 0.40x`. f32 versions of the same benchmarks are 0.74–0.98x — the bf16-vs-f32 ratio is the smoking gun. `softmax bf16` at 0.66–0.69x partly inherits the same issue.

**Why it's slow.** Decomposed `convert_bf16→f32`, scalar/narrow-vector f32 reduction, `convert_f32→bf16`. XLA's reduction emitter doesn't keep `vector<Nxf32>` accumulators while loading `vector<Nxbf16>` and widening on the fly.

**What to try.** In the reduction emitter, load `vector<Nxbf16>`, widen to `vector<Nxf32>`, accumulate in f32 vector registers, narrow once at the end. Standard BF16-DP pattern. The YNN follow-up showed disabling YNN REDUCE closes ~20% on bf16 — most of the remaining gap is the absent vectorized bf16 accumulator.

**Effort: S–M.** Localized to the reduction emitter (the f32 path already works).
**Payoff: high.** ~6 rows directly; on the critical path for any bf16 transformer activation.
**Confidence: high** that the diagnosis is right (f32/bf16 ratio asymmetry).

### 5. `gelu_chain f32` at 1024 — new regression visible only with proper threading
**Gap.** `gelu_chain f32 (1,1024,768)` = **0.30x** at 8 threads (was 0.65x at 32 threads; the threading fix exposed the real cost). Two identity matmuls with a GELU between them — identity matmul is essentially memcpy, so this is GELU + dispatch overhead at small shape.

**Why it's slow.** Plausible: at 1024×768 the work doesn't amortize XLA's parallel-task dispatch overhead, and the per-thread chunk is too small. The 32-thread number was effectively measuring single-threaded throughput because there's not enough work; pinning to 8 threads exposes the per-task overhead.

**What to try.** Compare HLO and parallel-task splits for this shape against torch's. If XLA is over-parallelizing, raise the min-chunk-size threshold for the parallel partitioner on small shapes.

**Effort: S.** Heuristic tuning.
**Payoff: medium.** Helps small-shape compounds; doesn't address the GEMM cliff at medium sizes.
**Confidence: medium.** Could also be a fusion-boundary issue around GELU that the bigger threading numbers were masking.

### 6. Misaligned-tail elementwise — still leaking ~30%
**Gap.** `add_mul_aligned_vs_misaligned`: 1024 = 0.84x, 1023 = 0.77x, **1025 = 0.68x** (worsened from 0.71x in the prior run — bigger gap at 8 threads). The 1025 case forces a scalar tail or poorly-masked vector tail.

**What to try.** Audit emitter output for d=1025 specifically vs d=1023. Likely fix: masked vector for the trailing 1-element tail rather than splitting into vector-body + scalar-tail.

**Effort: S** (in your existing xtile-aligned-ops territory).
**Payoff: medium.** ~30% on misaligned trailing dims; generalizes to any d_model not a multiple of vector width (vocab sizes, head_dim × num_heads).
**Confidence: high.**

### 7. f32 softmax — multi-pass max → exp → sum → div fusion
**Gap.** `softmax (1,12,512,512) f32 0.75x`, `(1,16,1024,1024) f32 0.74x`. Constant ~25% loss across shape. Cascades into `mha_block` and `gpt2_block`.

**What to try.** Two-pass-fused (subtract-max + exp inside the sum-reduction loop) or online-softmax (one pass with running max). PyTorch likely fuses max-then-sum into one template.

**Effort: M** (two-pass-fused), **L** (online-softmax).
**Payoff: medium–high.** Direct 25% on softmax rows; helps mha/gpt2.
**Confidence: high** that softmax is multi-pass today.

### 8. f32 pure-pointwise at "real activation" sizes (4096 rows)
**Gap.** Same op, two shapes: `add_chain 1.19x → 0.71x` (1024 → 4096), `rms_norm 1.43x → 0.71x` (same shape sweep). XLA wins at L2-resident sizes, loses at DRAM-resident sizes.

**Why it's slow.** Bandwidth-bound at 4 × 4096 × 768 × 4B = 48MB. Likely a parallel-chunk-size mismatch — XLA's split for memory-bound pointwise at 4096 doesn't amortize prefetch the way torch's does.

**What to try.** Tune the parallel-block partitioner for memory-bound pointwise at 4096 rows. Software prefetch hints in the vector loop.

**Effort: S–M.**
**Payoff: medium.** Helps a class of memory-bound pointwise/normalization at real-activation sizes.
**Confidence: medium.**

### 9. Bias + GELU + residual fusion into the GEMM epilogue
**Gap.** `dot_bias_gelu f32 = 0.47x`. The GEMM piece alone (`dot_medium`) is also slow at 0.16x, but the epilogue fusion question is independent: are bias + GELU riding the dot, or done as separate kernels?

**What to try.** Check HLO. If GELU is emitted as a separate post-dot kernel, plumb oneDNN `post_ops=eltwise_gelu_tanh` (or extend XLA's emitter to take an epilogue functor). Same hook should accept `relu`, `silu`, `bias_add`, `add(residual)`.

**Effort: M.** Dispatch + new fusion-pass rule.
**Payoff: high.** Residual-add at the end of GPT-2 / Mixer MLPs is the same pattern; critical-path for every transformer.
**Confidence: medium–high.**

### 10. GQA `repeat_interleave` materialization
**Gap.** `gqa_block (1,2048) f32 0.39x` (was 0.72x at uncapped XLA — bigger gap exposed). The expand-K/V step `jnp.repeat(k, 4, axis=1)` may be materializing a 4× tensor before the matmul.

**What to try.** Check HLO. If materialized, a fusion rule for dot-with-broadcast-input lets the broadcast happen in-stride. Cleaner long-term: rewrite GQA as an einsum exposing the group structure (`bhqsd,bhksd->bhqsk` with `h_q`/`h_kv` as separate axes).

**Effort: M.**
**Payoff: medium–high.** GQA is the attention shape in modern LLMs.
**Confidence: medium.**

### 11. expert_dispatch_combine f32 — dense MoE block
**Gap.** `expert_dispatch_combine (1,1024,768) f32 = 0.31x`. 16 stacked matmuls + GELU + gate. The wide intermediate (8 × 1 × 1024 × 3072 = 96MB f32) thrashes cache.

**What to try.** Mostly inherits from #1 (f32 GEMM) and #9 (epilogue fusion) once those land. Specific to MoE: a per-expert matmul-fuse pass that doesn't materialize the full `(E, B, S, H)` intermediate.

**Effort: M.** Composes with other fixes.
**Payoff: high if you care about MoE.** Load-bearing for modern LLMs.
**Confidence: medium.** Diagnosis correct; isolated payoff size depends on what #1 and #9 deliver.

### 12. Conv variants — grouped and 3×3 baseline
**Gap.** `grouped_conv_3x3 f32 0.65x` (likely falls off XLA's oneDNN dispatch for `groups>1`). `conv2d_3x3_resnet f32 0.58x` — both sides should hit oneDNN but XLA loses by ~40%.

**What to try.** Verify dispatch: does XLA's conv path reach oneDNN for `groups=4`? If not, route grouped convs through per-group oneDNN calls. For 3×3 baseline f32 at 0.58x, audit oneDNN dispatch hints.

**Effort: S–M.**
**Payoff: medium.** ResNeXt + MobileNet-derived models hit grouped/depthwise heavily, but those models aren't on the LLM critical path.
**Confidence: medium.**

## Cross-cutting note

Items #2 (sort), #3 (layer_norm/YNN), #4 (bf16 reduce), #7 (softmax) all touch reductions or sort/scan-like emitters. The "shared multi-output reduction primitive" hypothesis from the original cross-cutting note still stands — a single fused-multi-output-reduction primitive that supports `(sum_x, sum_x², max, ...)` with bf16-vector loads and f32-vector accumulators would unlock most of items #3, #4, #7, and lay foundation for flash-attention. Items #1 (GEMM dispatch) and #9 (epilogue fusion) form a separate logical cluster that lights up every transformer.

## Methodology note

This sweep is the first one with correct XLA threading via `PJRT_NPROC=8`. Earlier sweeps had XLA at 32 cores while PyTorch was capped at 8, biasing speedups in XLA's favor by 5–50% depending on the benchmark. The qualitative findings from the prior runs hold (layer_norm cliff, sort gap, YNN-REDUCE-specific regression) but the absolute speedup numbers — especially XLA-wins — were inflated. Treat the 2026-06-04 numbers as the new baseline; the 2026-06-02 perf-wins doc's diagnosis is still correct, its ordering needs the GEMM and sort items moved up.

## Update 2026-06-04 — investigation footnotes

Two follow-up investigations triggered by the consolidated sweep findings.

### ARM dispatch finding

Question: is XLA dispatching to oneDNN on Apple Silicon?

**Answer: no.** `XLA_ONEDNN` is defined only for Linux x86_64, Linux aarch64+MKL, and Windows (`xla/tsl/mkl/build_defs.bzl:31-43`). macOS aarch64 hits `conditions:default` and the entire oneDNN code path is dead. f32 dots route through `LibraryRewriter` → `IsDotSupportedByYnn` → `__ynn_fusion` kCustom → XNNPACK NEON GEMM. Verified via HLO dump from installed jaxlib 0.10.1 (`cpu_after_optimizations.txt` shows `"fusion_config":{"kind":"__ynn_fusion"}`, zero `__onednn` refs) and by disabling YNN dot dispatch and falling through to Eigen (`__xla_cpu_runtime_EigenMatMulF32`) at the same ~800 GFLOPS — both are NEON.

Apple AMX (the coprocessor PyTorch hits at ~5 TFLOPS via Accelerate/BNNS) is not reachable from any current XLA backend. The only fix to close the Apple GEMM gap is a new Accelerate/BNNS backend, gated on `__APPLE__`. Substantial integration work, Apple-only, out of perf-wins scope. **Critical implication: dot_medium 0.16x is an Apple-only number; x86 has a totally different dispatch path and needs its own sweep before any GEMM-targeted work.**

### YNN-reduce finding

Question: is there a YNNPACK bug affecting f32 reductions?

**Answer: no correctness bug; the team's own TODO matches the perf finding.** Source review of `ynn_emitter.cc::DefineReduceOp`, `ynnpack.h` reduce semantics, and the matcher in `ynn_support.cc::IsReduceLikeOpOffloadedToYnn` shows correct math (init broadcast then accumulate, matches XLA Reduce semantics including non-identity init, no variadic/reducer-op/dim-selection bugs).

The "bug" is structural — `LibraryRewriter` (line 1049) runs before `CpuInstructionFusion` (line 1059) in `cpu_compiler.cc`, and `IsCustomFusion()` instructions are added to the fusion-skip set, so YNN-wrapped reduces can no longer fuse into their downstream elementwise consumers. The existing `IsReduceLikeOpOffloadedToYnn` exclusion (input-opcode based) doesn't cover the consumer side. This is exactly what the layer_norm 3× toggle-result predicts, and the comments in the code (`TODO(b/441837668)`, "for now they are excluded because the codegen for the fusion with reduce can be faster") indicate the team knows the heuristic is provisional.

The fix landed as item #3 above; recommendation is to file an upstream issue rather than land a one-line heuristic blind on x86 (the rms_norm-small / softmax-bf16 toggle "wins" are likely fusion-side-effect noise, not YNN-REDUCE contributions, so they're not a constraint on the fix — but YNN's reduce kernel quality differs by ISA and the heuristic should be measured on x86 before committing).
